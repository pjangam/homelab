#### Feasibility
**Yes, all five options are ordinary builds or ordinary purchases.** Nothing here is new engineering.
- **Ceiling-hung box nets** on four corner hooks are the standard Indian mosquito net; a cord that goes up through a pulley and down to a cleat is the same mechanism as a pulley clothes airer ([This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/laundry/pulley-style-drying-rack)).
- **Foldable zip tent nets** are widely sold in India, about ₹250-2,500 (search snapshot, 2026-11-26): e.g. a Flipkart "Classic" 200x200x145cm foldable at ₹999 ([listing](https://www.flipkart.com/classic-net-double-bed-mosquito/p/itme5vaxphcmvkfp)), a Tri-Activ king foldable on bigbasket at ₹889 ([listing](https://www.bigbasket.com/pd/40340072)). "King size" labels vary: several are really 6x6ft.
- **The one real constraint is the baby.** Cord loops strangle: Health Canada notes a 22cm cord or a 44cm loop is enough ([Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/home-safety/blind-cord-safety.html)); CPSC says cordless is the only design that removes the hazard, and that cord above a reachable cleat is still a hazard ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Window-Covering)); UK guidance puts cleats at 1.5m or higher ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/child-safety-blind-cords)).

#### Design notes
- **Recommended: A, curtain-style frame.** It is the only option where opening is a slide, not untucking, and it has no pull cord for a baby to reach. Fall back to **E** if drilling is not allowed or the room may change.
- **Frame, not wire:** a tensioned curtain wire cannot turn the corners of a bed, so the frame is four lengths of 3/4in CPVC (or 19mm aluminium curtain rod) joined by 90-degree elbows. Hang it at the elbows, so rings on each side slide freely between corners; each side's panel stays on its own side.
- **Entry side:** two panels meeting in the middle with a 30cm overlap, or one panel that slides to the corner. The other three sides can be one fixed panel each, overlapping 20-30cm at the corners so there is no gap.
- **Hem: weighted, not tucked.** Tucking under the mattress is what makes nets slow to open, and it stops a curtain sliding. Make the panels long enough to rest 15-20cm on the mattress top or reach the floor, with curtain weight tape in the hem so they hang straight and close the gap.
- **The net touching the sleeper:** mosquitoes bite through a net that rests on skin. Hang the frame 10-15cm outside the mattress edge on every side, and keep the roof taut. That is why the frame beats a single-point cone over a double bed.
- **Ceiling fan clearance:** the frame and roof must sit well below the blades, at least 30cm, or the fan lifts and flutters the roof and can catch it. With a 1,200mm fan centred over the bed, the corner hooks fall outside the sweep in plan, but the height is the trap: measure the blade height first (open items). Expect less breeze through any net; that cost applies to every option.
- **Ceiling type decides the anchors:**
  - **RCC slab:** 6mm or 8mm hammer drill bit, rawl plug and a screw-in eye hook or ceiling hook. Loads are small (a net plus a frame is a couple of kg).
  - **False ceiling (gypsum or PVC):** the board alone will not hold a pulled cord; anchor into the slab above with a threaded rod or a long hook through the board.
  - **No drilling:** E, or a free-standing four-post frame (not costed, it is furniture).
- **Cord safety, if option B or C with a pulley is picked:**
  - Cleat at 1.6m or higher on the wall, **not** beside the bed or cot, where a toddler standing on the mattress can reach.
  - No loops: one free tail end, never a knotted loop or a continuous loop; trim the tail to just reach the cleat.
  - Better still, a **tensioned or breakaway** design: a cord tensioner fixed low (as on continuous-loop blinds) or a breakaway connector that parts under a child's weight.
  - Tie-backs, too, are fixed at both ends and short, so they never form a loop that hangs free.
- **Washability:** polyester net, washed every month or two. A needs the panels to come off the rings (use clip rings or split rings, not sewn-on loops); B and C unhook from the corners; E is awkward to wash with its sprung frame inside.

#### Readymade alternatives
- **Foldable zip tent nets (option E):** ₹900-1,500 for a decent one on Flipkart or bigbasket (search snapshot above); about ₹250-400 for the wholesale-looking ones, which tend to be thin. Check the real dimensions against the mattress and that it has zips on two sides.
- **Ready box and round canopy nets** are sold at local cloth and bedding shops; they cover B and C without stitching.
- **Ready curtain-style bed canopies** (a frame with sliding net panels) were not found sold as a kit in India; A has to be assembled. Four-poster beds with net rails are furniture, not this project.
- **Rejected:** a motorised hoist. It is the same cord with a motor attached, adds an outage to a bedtime routine on a power-cut-prone house, and solves nothing that a slide does not.
