Kandil Relay Wiring

One ESP32 devkit switching one SONGLE SRD-05VDC-SL-C relay, which switches the akash kandil's 230V bulb on the Diwali timer. The relay is either on a blue module or a bare cube, and the repo does not say which, so both are drawn. The page splits into two halves. The low-voltage half (USB, ESP32, switch, relay coil) is safe to get wrong and try again. The mains half is wired with the plug out, checked with a meter, and closed in a plastic box before it ever goes into a socket.

The whole circuit

The ESP32 side is the same in both variants: USB power in, GPIO26 out to the relay, a switch from GPIO27 to ground, and the board's VIN/5V pin feeding the relay coil. The ESP32 cannot drive the coil from a GPIO: the coil wants about 70mA at 5V, against roughly 20mA from a 3.3V pin. So something sits in between. On a module it is already on the board (variant A). On a bare cube you build it from one NPN transistor, one resistor and one diode (variant B).

Which one is yours: if the relay is soldered to a small PCB (usually blue) with a 3-pin header on one side and three screw terminals on the other, it is A. If it is just the cube with five pins underneath, it is B.

A module that clicks on and never lets goMost blue modules are low-level trigger: IN pulled to ground lights an optocoupler LED fed from VCC. With VCC at 5V, the ESP32's "off" level is only 3.3V, which leaves 1.7V across that LED and its resistor. With no indicator LED in that path, about 0.5mA still flows, and through an opto with a 50%+ transfer ratio that can be enough to keep the driver transistor on. If the relay sticks on or chatters: on a module with a JD-VCC jumper, take the cap off, feed VCC from the ESP32's 3V3 and JD-VCC from VIN (inset above). Without that jumper, use variant B with the same cube, or a high-trigger module with relay_inverted: "false".

The diode backwards is a shortStripe on the collector side instead of the 5V side, and the diode conducts the moment the transistor turns on: 5V straight into the transistor with only the board's USB fuse and the diode's own resistance in the way. The transistor or the board's 5V path dies. Check the stripe against the drawing before the first power-up.

Finding the pins and legs

On the ESP32, all four pins are on the same header row on both common devkits. Hold it antenna up, USB down. On a 30-pin DOIT-style board, the row with D26 reads down D25, D26, D27, D14, D12, D13, GND, VIN. On a 38-pin DevKitC it reads 25, 26, 27, 14, 12, GND, 13, D2, D3, CMD, 5V. The silkscreen may say D26, IO26, G26 or just 26. The repo does not record which board layout the spare is, so read the print rather than counting.

The load side

The relay is just a switch in the live wire. Live goes from the plug through a fuse to COM; NO goes on to the bulb holder; the bulb's other contact returns on neutral. Neutral and earth run straight through, joined with lever connectors. NC stays empty. Wired to NO, a dead ESP32 or a lost relay supply means the lamp is off, never stuck on.

No-mains alternative: a USB LED string

If the kandil gets a 5V USB LED string instead of a bulb, the relay switches it on the same two contacts. Cut a USB cable (or the string's own lead), and put its +5V core (usually red) through COM and NO. Ground (usually black) runs straight through. Use a second charger or a power bank for the string, so the string's current never passes through the ESP32's VIN pin. Nothing on this side can hurt you, so no box or fuse is needed. It also makes a good first test of the contacts before the mains wiring is built.

Mains safety

Plug out for every mains stepEvery screw, strip and meter check on the 230V side happens with the plug out of the wall, not just the socket switch off. The box lid goes on before the plug goes in. If anything needs changing, unplug first, then open the box.

Keep the two halves apartThe ESP32, its USB cable and the switch go in one corner of the box; the fuse, relay contacts and lever connectors go in the other, at least a few centimetres apart, with the mains cores cable-tied down so a loose one cannot swing across. The mains cable is clamped where it enters (gland, grommet plus tie, or a knot inside), so a tug on the cable pulls on the box, not on the screw terminals. The USB cable is the only thing that leaves the low-voltage corner. Nothing with a metal case or exposed metal goes in.

Use a 3-pin plug, not a 2-pin oneIndian 2-pin sockets are not polarised: a 2-pin plug goes in either way round, and half the time the relay switches neutral instead of live. The bulb still goes off, but the holder stays at 230V with the lamp "off". A 3-pin plug only fits one way, so the core on its L pin really is live, assuming the socket itself is wired correctly. Brown (or red) is live, blue (or black) neutral, green/yellow earth.

A 60W bulb in a paper lantern, unattendedAn incandescent bulb turns nearly all of its 60W into heat, and its glass runs well over 100°C. Inside a paper kandil, on a timer, with nobody in the room, that is the one real fire risk in this build, and the relay does not change it. Hang the bulb so it sits in the middle of the lantern, well clear of the paper on every side, with the opening at the top and bottom left free so heat can rise out. After the first hour, feel the paper nearest the bulb: warm is the limit, hot means more clearance. Keep the timer's on-window to the evening, and do not leave it running while nobody is home. Simplest fix: a 7–9W LED bulb (~₹100–150) in the same B22 holder - same wiring, same fuse, a fraction of the heat.

The mains rating of a cheap moduleThe cube is rated 10A 250VAC, but a hobby module's traces and screw terminals are the weaker link. At 0.26A that does not matter. It does if this box is ever reused for a heater, geyser or iron: it is for lights and a fan, nothing heavier.

The numbers

Relay coil. The SRD-05VDC-SL-C coil is ~70Ω: 71mA at 5V, about 67mA at the ~4.7V the VIN pin typically gives. It picks up at 75% of rated (3.75V) or more, so 4.7V closes it reliably. Base drive (B). (3.3V − 0.7V) / 1k = 2.6mA from GPIO26, well inside the ESP32's ~20mA per pin. For 67mA of coil current that is a forced gain of about 26, enough to saturate either transistor. The BC547 is rated 100mA collector, so it runs at about two thirds of its limit; the 2N2222 (600mA) has more headroom and is the better pick if both are in the drawer. USB budget. The ESP32 peaks at roughly 250mA on WiFi transmit, plus 70mA of coil, so ~320mA in all: any 1A charger, or a laptop port, covers it.

The bulb. 60W / 230V = 0.26A steady. A cold filament has about a tenth of its hot resistance (880Ω hot), so at switch-on it draws ~10x that, about 2.6A, for a few milliseconds while it heats. The 10A contacts take that easily. A T2A slow-blow fuse rides through the inrush and still blows on a short. A 1A fast fuse can blow on the inrush alone, so don't use one. 0.75mm² flexible cable is rated well above 2A.

Verify in this order

Each step adds one thing, so a failure points at that thing. Steps 1-7 are all low voltage and safe to repeat. The mains wiring is built only after step 7 passes.

  1. no powerMeter the relay. Resistance between pins (or module terminals). Expected: two pins read ~70Ω (coil, B only); COM to one end pin ~0Ω (NC); COM to the other open (NO). No ~70Ω pair = you are on a module's screw side or the coil is open; nothing reads 0Ω = you have not found COM yet.
  2. USB only, nothing attachedFlash and add to HA (./esphome.sh run --device /dev/ttyUSB0), then toggle Relay in HA. Expected: the blue onboard LED follows. LED does not follow = firmware or HA, not wiring; read ./esphome.sh logs. Nothing external is on the board yet, so wiring cannot be the cause.
  3. USB, switch onlyWire the switch (or a jumper wire) between GPIO27 and GND, and flip it. Expected: the blue LED toggles and HA's Wall switch changes. Nothing = wrong pin; check the silkscreen. This test is harmless whichever pin you hit by mistake, as long as the other end is GND: never jumper a GPIO to VIN/5V, which is not 3.3V-safe.
  4. unplug USB, then wireAdd the relay driver (A or B), no load. Plug USB back in and watch through a reset. Expected: no click at boot; toggling in HA gives one click per toggle and the module's LED (A) follows. A: relay is on when HA says off = a high-trigger module; set relay_inverted: "false". A: clicks on and never off = the 3.3V low-trigger trap; use the JD-VCC split. B: no click at all = transistor legs (check E and C against the leg diagram), or the diode backwards (a warm transistor says this; unplug at once). B: clicks at every reset = fit the optional 10k.
  5. USB, meter on contactsContinuity on COM-NO while toggling. Expected: beeps when on, silent when off. The reverse = you are on COM-NC.
  6. USBWithout the network: unplug the router (or block the board's WiFi) and flip the switch. Expected: the relay still toggles. Then pull USB with the relay on and plug it back in. Expected: it comes back on.
  7. optional, low voltageContacts under load: the USB LED string on COM/NO from its own charger. Expected: the string follows the relay. String on when off = it is on NC.
  8. plug out of the wallBuild the mains side, then meter it from the plug pins, with the bulb fitted and the ESP32 on USB. Plug L to N, relay off: open. Relay on: about 60-90Ω (the 60W bulb's cold filament; hot it would be 880Ω). E to L and E to N: open in both states. Reads 0Ω when on = a short across the holder; do not plug in. Open when on = the fuse, a loose terminal, or the bulb. Reads through when off = the lamp is on NC.
  9. lid on, then plug inFirst mains power. Toggle from HA and from the switch. Expected: the bulb follows the relay. Fuse blows at switch-on = unplug, then look for a short at the holder; a T2A should not blow on the inrush. Then let it run an hour inside the kandil before trusting it to the timer, and feel the paper.

Safe probingEvery meter check above is made with the plug out of the wall: resistance and continuity on the bench, or from the plug's own pins with the box closed. Nothing on this page asks for a probe on live 230V. On the low-voltage side the only supply is USB 5V, and every test either goes through the board's own pins or is a switch to ground.
