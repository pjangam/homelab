Aarti Strip Wiring

180 WS2812B pixels behind the makhar, an INMP441 mic on the same ESP32, and a 5V 4A supply that reaches the strip over mains-wire offcuts and never through a breadboard. The joint everything depends on is the common ground: when it opened on this build, every pixel lit and cycled colours while WLED was commanding solid red — it looked like an effect, not a fault.

The whole circuit

One drawing, the whole build: mic, board, strip and supply. Two power sources — the ESP32 on its own USB adapter, the strip on the 5V 4A — meeting at exactly two places, the data line and the ground joint that gives that data line a reference.

FromToWhy it matters
supply +5V strip +5V pad Direct, on mains-wire offcut. Never the ESP32's 5V pin, and never through a breadboard: jumper wire and contact resistance cap this path at roughly 1A, which on this build made three different supplies look identically dim.
supply GND strip GND pad The return for the same 3.4A. Same wire class as +5V.
ESP32 GND that same ground wire The common ground. Two supplies with no shared ground leaves the data line with no reference: pixels latch noise every refresh, so all 180 light and cycle colours while WLED commands solid red. It reads as an effect, not a fault.
ESP32 GPIO4 strip DIN, through 470R Series resistor damps reflections on the data edge. GPIO4 is free on both WROOM and WROVER and is not a strapping pin; WLED's own default is GPIO2, and this board shipped defaulting to GPIO16, so the pin is set explicitly in LED settings.
1000µF across the strip's input pads Bulk charge for the current step when 180 pixels jump brightness together. Polarised: the striped leg goes to GND.

The mic bus, on the same board

The INMP441 is an I2S digital microphone: it hands the ESP32 a clock, a channel select and a data stream rather than an analogue voltage. Six wires, one of which is only a tie to ground. It shares the board's 3.3V and ground and touches nothing on the strip side. SD is the only line the mic drives; the ESP32 drives WS and SCK, which is the whole reason those two cannot sit on GPIO34–39.

Mic pinESP32What it does
SDGPIO32Serial data — the audio itself, mic to ESP32.
WSGPIO25Word select. Driven by the ESP32.
SCKGPIO33Bit clock. Driven by the ESP32.
VDD3V33.3V part. 5V damages it.
GNDGNDSame ground as the board and, through the common-ground joint, the strip.
L/RGNDChannel select, tied low for the left channel. Floating gives a mic that reads flat zero.

Changing a mic pin needs a rebootWLED initialises I2S at boot, so writing digitalmic.pin on a running board leaves the driver on the old pins and the mic looking dead. POST /json/state {"rb":true}, and note that the uptime read immediately afterwards can still be the pre-reboot value.

Two things on this page are inference, not recordThe repo records the strip's supply exactly; it does not say in so many words how the ESP32 ends up powered in the finished build, or whether the bench 1000µF was carried onto the perfboard. Both are drawn the way the runbook wired them at the bench (ESP32 on its own USB adapter, cap across the strip input) because nothing in the build notes changed either. If the board is instead fed from the 5V 4A supply, that is fine electrically — it is inside the 600mA left under the 3400mA cap — and the common ground then already exists through the supply. Check the actual harness before trusting this box, and correct the page.

The numbers behind the current cap

WLED's limiter is what makes this safe, not the supply's rating. It is configured with 55mA per LED at full white, so it models 180 pixels as 9.9A flat out — far past the 4A supply. The cap of 3400mA makes that unreachable: WLED scales brightness down until the estimate fits.

LEDs 180 type WS281x data pin 4 order GRB mA/LED 55 max current 3400mA supply 5V 4A

Measured against that: all-red at full brightness drew 3411mA and nothing ran warm. All-red is the worst case one die at a time — 180 × ~18.3mA — which is why it lands almost exactly on the cap. Resting with the renderer holding black, WLED estimates ~300mA: its fixed overhead plus 180 idle pixels, not the ~120mA of a board that is switched off.

What is deliberately absent

Not fittedWhy not, on this build
74AHCT125 3.3V data into a 5V strip is out of spec — WS2812B wants 0.7×VDD, i.e. 3.5V — and it works reliably at this 3m length. It was bought and never needed. If the first pixel ever flickers or shows a wrong colour while the rest are right, this is the fix, not a new strip.
far-end injection The pink-toward-the-far-end drift that injection cures is a 5m problem. At 3m, capped at 3400mA and bounced off a wall, no drift was visible. Re-check if the run is ever extended with the ~2m of spare reel.
breadboard / duponts in the power path Removed on purpose at Phase 3. They are the ~1A ceiling described above.

The strip end, as a physical part

The one view that does not belong in the schematic above, because it is about copper and silkscreen rather than nets. Worth having when re-terminating a joint, or when the ~2m of spare reel gets cut for something else: trace width identifies the data line with no meter and no readable label, and the printed arrows decide which end is the input.

Verify in this order

This is a diagnostic order, not a build order: each step isolates a different fault, so a failure says where to look. Run them against a strip that is already known to have worked.

  1. Ask the board what it thinks it is. ./projects/aarti-lights/wled.sh info should report 180 LEDs, version 16.0.1 and data pin 4. A wrong count or pin is a settings problem and no amount of rewiring fixes it — WLED has defaulted this board's pin to GPIO16 before.
  2. Solid red at low brightness. All 180 steady = power, data and the common ground are all good. All 180 lit but cycling colours on their own = the common ground is open; that is this build's signature failure and it looks like an effect. The first N lit and the rest dark = the data chain breaks at pixel N, so look at that solder joint or cut, not at the supply.
  3. Red, then green, then blue. Red rendering red confirms GRB order. Red and green swapped is the colour-order setting, never the wiring.
  4. Full brightness, all red, and read the estimated current. It should sit near 3400mA — 3411mA measured on this build — and nothing should get warm. Dim, and barely changing as brightness rises, means the current path is limited: a jumper or breadboard has crept back into the power run, or the cap was lowered.
  5. Clap at the mic with ./projects/aarti-lights/wled-audio-monitor.py running. Levels moving = the I2S bus is alive. A flat zero is almost always WS or SCK on an input-only pin, the mic on 5V, or L/R floating — and remember a pin change does nothing until the board reboots.
  6. Confirm who is driving the pixels. /json/info showing live: true with lm: UDP means the Tier 3 renderer on xero has the strip; live: false means WLED's own effects do. A strip that ignores the renderer but responds to the web UI is a service problem, not a wiring one.

Safe probingEverything above is read from the network or the WLED UI, so nothing needs a probe on a live 3.4A rail. If a meter does come out, measure voltage across the strip's input pads with the strip running — not continuity, which needs the supply off, and never a bare wire from the supply to a pad.
