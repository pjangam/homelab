Diwali Door Wiring

284 WS2812B pixels around the front door in two pieces: the 180-pixel aarti strip up the near side and across the top, and the ~104-pixel reel offcut down the far side. An INMP441 mic on the same board makes it react to firecrackers. One 5V 4A supply feeds the board, the strip's start and a power-injection pair to the joint at the far top corner. The thing that matters most is that joint: three wires from piece A's output end to piece B's input end, with the injection pair landing on the same +5V and GND. Swapping the injection pair at the joint shorts the supply.

The whole circuit

The supply's output splits three ways at the controller box: the strip's start, the ESP32's VIN pin, and the injection pair that runs ~3m along the frame to the joint. Data enters at the start and flows through piece A, across the joint and down piece B. The mic is borrowed from a voice satellite for the festival week. If no mic is fitted, WLED still runs, just not sound-reactive.

The mic, on the same board

The INMP441 drives firecracker-reactive mode. It is borrowed from one of the two voice satellites for the festival week, and it uses the same pins as the aarti build. It runs on the board's 3.3V and ground and does not touch the strip side. Mount it at the door's outer side, facing outdoors, on a short lead from the ESP32 box. Keep the lead as short as the mounting allows: SCK and WS are clock lines, and a long unshielded run can pick up noise. Out of the box, it is on the weather side of the door, so keep it under the frame's overhang and away from rain.

The joint, as a physical part

Update 2026-10-28: A and B are already soldered together, so the strip is one 284-pixel piece and the joint is done. What that changes: (1) mount it so the joint lands at the far top corner, which it does when the strip starts at the bottom of the socket side (A is exactly 3m: 2.1m up plus 0.9m across); (2) if the joint is butted pad-to-pad rather than linked by short wires, do not bend at the joint - make the 90° turn a few centimetres past it, by folding the strip over itself at 45° (the flat strip folds cleanly; it does not bend sideways); (3) the injection pair still lands on the joint's +5V and GND pads, which is one more solder job on an existing joint. Alternative with no soldering: if B's far end is the reel's original end, it usually carries a factory pigtail with a spare red/white power pair - land the injection pair there instead (the far end, ~1.7m past the corner, so the pair runs ~5m along the frame instead of ~3m). Check the arrows before mounting: they must point away from the supply along the whole strip. The text below describes making the joint and is kept for reference.

At the corner, piece A's output end meets piece B's input end. Piece B turns 90° to go down the jamb, so join them with three short wires rather than butting the pads together. Identify each end by its copper, not by wire colour. The printed arrows on both pieces must point the same way, away from the supply.

The injection pair, swapped, is a short across the supplyThe pair is ~3m long and both cores look alike at the far end. Mark the +5V core at both ends before routing it, with red tape or a knot. Then do the resistance check in step 3 with everything unplugged before you apply power. A brick with short protection usually shuts off and survives, but do not count on it.

USB and VIN at the same timeOn this build the board's 5V pin carries the brick's 5V. With a USB cable plugged in too, two 5V sources meet on that rail. Many devkits have a diode between USB and VIN, but some do not. A board without one can push current back into the laptop's USB port. Flash on the bench with the supply unplugged, or unplug the supply before you connect USB. After the first flash, update over Wi-Fi (WLED's OTA page) and leave USB out.

3.3V data into a 5V strip, no level shifterWS2812B wants about 0.7 × 5V = 3.5V for a logic high, and the ESP32 gives 3.3V. That is out of spec, but it worked reliably on the aarti build with this strip, this resistor and a similar short lead, so no shifter is fitted here. If pixel 1 flickers or shows the wrong colour while the rest are right, the fix is a 74AHCT125, not a new strip. Keep the GPIO4 to DIN lead short so the box sits at the strip start.

The power numbers

A WS2812B pixel draws up to ~60mA at full white (20mA per die). 284 × 60mA = ~17A, more than four times the brick's 4A. WLED's current limiter is the only thing that keeps the brick safe. At 3400mA, it dims the strip until its estimate fits. That leaves ~600mA under the 4A rating for the ESP32, which draws roughly 100-250mA with Wi-Fi active, plus margin. Warm, dim effects such as Candle Multi stay far below the cap. Only a full-white or full-brightness preset reaches it.

The injection cable: 0.75mm² copper is about 0.023Ω per metre. A 3m run is ~0.14Ω for the loop (out and back). Even if all 3.4A went through it, the drop would be ~0.5V. In practice it carries the share for the far side, roughly half, so ~0.25V. That is within what the strip tolerates. Without the cable, B's current would have to flow through the full 3m of A's thin copper traces first.

The script does this correctly; step 4 of aarti_lights_setup.md said 0x0 until it was corrected on 2026-10-28. xero has a dead USB port, so flash from a known-good port or the 2019 MacBook.

  • Boot preset 1: a warm effect, either Candle Multi or Fire Flicker in amber. The strip then comes back correctly after a power cut without HA or xero.
  • Mic setup. Enable the AudioReactive usermod, which ships disabled, and set the I2S pins to SD 32, WS 25, SCK 33. Reboot after any pin change, because WLED only initialises I2S at boot.
  • Firecracker mode preset. A peak-triggered effect, Ripple Peak or Puddlepeak, on a warm or gold palette. Set squelch high so only bangs trigger it. Tune the thresholds on the first evening.
  • Fireworks preset (no sound needed). Starburst or Fireworks, as the fallback when the mic is out or the reactive preset misbehaves.

Verify in this order

This is a build order and a diagnostic order together. Each step adds one thing, so a failure points at that one thing. Do steps 1-4 on the bench, before anything goes on the frame.

  1. Board alone, on USB, supply unplugged. Flash, join Wi-Fi, set the static IP, then run WLED_HOST=192.168.1.127 ./projects/aarti-lights/wled.sh info. Set the data pin to 4, order GRB and the cap to 3400. Expected: it answers and reports pin 4. A wrong pin or count is a settings problem, not a wiring one.
  2. Unplug USB. Supply to the box, the board and piece A only. Set count 180, solid red, low brightness. Expected: all 180 steady red. All lit but cycling colours = the common ground is open. First N lit, rest dark = the data chain breaks at pixel N. Nothing lit, board on Wi-Fi = the data line: GPIO4, the 470R, or DOUT used instead of DIN. Board not on Wi-Fi = VIN/GND to the board.
  3. Supply unplugged: meter check of the injection pair. Make the joint (A to B, three links) and land the injection pair on it. Measure resistance from the supply-end +5V core to the box's +5V, then from the supply-end GND core to the box's GND. Expected: near 0Ω for each. Then measure across +5V and GND at the box. Expected: a reading that climbs as the capacitor charges, not one stuck at 0Ω. A steady 0Ω across +5V/GND = the pair is swapped somewhere. Do not power up.
  4. Power on, count 180 + B's count, solid red. Expected: every pixel on both pieces red. A lit, B dark = data does not reach B's DIN: B is the wrong way round, or the DO-DI link is open. B's first pixel wrong, rest fine = a marginal data joint at the corner. Count the lit pixels on B and correct the LED count.
  5. Red, then green, then blue. Red showing red confirms GRB. Red and green swapped = the colour-order setting, never the wiring.
  6. Full white at full brightness, briefly. Expected: the estimate sits at or under 3400mA, nothing warm, and B's far end looks the same white as A's start. B's far end pink or orange = the injection pair is not carrying current: an open joint at either end. If a meter comes out, measure DC volts across the joint's +5V and GND with the strip running, never continuity on a live rail.
  7. Pull the supply and plug it back. Expected: within a few seconds the strip comes back on its own in boot preset 1. Dark or a different effect = the boot preset is not saved.
  8. Mic: load the firecracker preset and clap close to the mic. Expected: a ripple on a loud clap, nothing on talking. Flat with any sound = WS or SCK on the wrong pin, the mic on 5V, L/R floating, or pins changed without a reboot. Triggers on speech = squelch too low.

Safe probingCheck resistance and continuity only with the supply unplugged. Check voltage only with the meter across pads, set to DC volts. Never bridge a bare wire from the supply to a pad to "test" a section; use the WLED UI to light it instead.
