Voice Satellite Wiring

The packed-away aarti ESP32 becomes an ESPHome voice satellite: the same INMP441 mic on the same three pins, an 8-LED WS2812B status light on GPIO4, and nothing but a USB charger for power. The one thing that can cost a part is the mic's supply. The INMP441 is a 3.3V part, so it goes on the 3V3 rail, and on this breadboard no rail carries 5V at all.

The whole circuit

One board, one supply. The USB charger feeds the ESP32, and the board's VIN pin passes that USB 5V on to the LED stick over a single wire. The mic is the aarti build's mic bus unchanged.

FromToWhy it matters
USB charger ESP32 USB socket The only supply. Any 5V charger rated 1A or more. A laptop's USB 2 port gives 500mA, which is enough for flashing but not for the stick at full white (see the numbers below).
ESP32 3V3 mic VDD, via the top + rail 3.3V only. The INMP441's absolute maximum supply is 3.63V. A mic fed 5V by mistake is likely dead, and it fails silently as a flat zero.
ESP32 GND mic GND, mic L/R, stick GND One supply, so this is one ground by construction. On a breadboard it still has to be continuous: bridge the top and bottom GND rails, and bridge each rail across its middle if your breadboard splits them.
ESP32 VIN / 5V stick +5V, one jumper USB 5V passed through the board. Many dev boards put a diode between USB and this pin, so expect roughly 4.7V. The pin is labelled VIN on some boards and 5V on others; it is the one beside a GND, never 3V3.
ESP32 GPIO4 stick DIN, through 470R The same resistor and pin as the aarti build. GPIO4 is not a strapping pin (those are 0, 2, 5, 12, 15) and is output-capable. The 470R damps the data edge and limits current into the first pixel to about 6mA if its supply is ever disconnected while data is running.

On the breadboard

The rail plan is the part to get right before anything else goes in. The top pair is 3V3 and GND; the bottom pair has GND only. The bottom + rail stays empty because 5V has one destination, the stick, and a single jumper from VIN gets it there. A 5V rail would add nothing except a place to plug the mic in by mistake.

The mic goes on 3V3. Check the rail before it goes inThe INMP441 is rated for 3.3V, with an absolute maximum of 3.63V; 5V can kill it. It fails quietly, as a flat zero that looks exactly like a wiring fault. Wire the board's 3V3 pin to the top + rail, plug in USB, and measure that rail against GND with a meter before the mic is on the breadboard. It must read 3.2-3.35V. If it reads around 5V, the jumper is on VIN/5V; move it, then measure again.

Breadboard rails can be split in the middleMany full-size breadboards break each rail halfway along, with a gap in the printed line. Two taps in different halves look connected and are not. That was the aarti build's ground fault. Keep every rail tap in one half, or add a short bridging jumper across the break on each rail you use.

The dev board may cover the breadboardMost ESP32 dev boards are wide enough that, straddling the centre gap, they leave one free hole per pin on one side and none on the other. If yours does that, use female-to-male jumpers on the covered side's pins from above, or set the board across two breadboards placed side by side.

Cutting the 8 LEDs

Take them from the ~2m spare offcut. Do not cut the 180-LED aarti strip. Cut from the offcut's input end, so the stick's first LED is the one the leads go to, and the arrows printed on the strip point away from the leads.

The numbers

Everything runs from one USB charger, so the budget is the charger and the board's USB path. A WS2812B draws about 60mA at full white (20mA per colour die), so 8 LEDs flat out are 480mA. The ESP32 with Wi-Fi streaming audio averages around 100mA, with transmit peaks near 240mA. The INMP441 takes under 2mA.

Logic level. WS2812B wants a data high of 0.7 × its supply. At the ~4.7V VIN gives after the board's diode, that is 3.3V, which the ESP32's 3.3V output just meets; at a full 5.0V it is 3.5V, slightly out of spec. The same strip runs reliably from 3.3V data over 3m on the aarti build, so a stick a few centimetres from the board needs no level shifter. The bulk capacitor is left out for the same reason: 8 LEDs switching at once is a step of at most ~0.5A, which the charger and the board's own capacitors handle.

For the ESPHome YAML, which does not exist yet. These are the values the wiring fixes; everything else is firmware choice.

i2s_lrclk_pin GPIO25 i2s_bclk_pin GPIO33 i2s_din_pin GPIO32 channel left light pin GPIO4 chipset WS2812 num_leds 8 rgb_order GRB

GRB is what the aarti strip from the same reel turned out to be: red rendered red with GRB set.

Drawn from the evidence, not checked on the boardThe repo records the chip (ESP32-D0WD-V3, 4MB) but not which dev-board layout it is, so this page does not show where pins sit along the header: find them by silkscreen, which may read D32, IO32, P32 or just 32. The USB-to-VIN diode and the resulting ~4.7V are typical of these boards, not measured on this one; check 2 below measures it. The aarti runbook moved the mic and board from the breadboard to a perfboard, so the mic may still be soldered there. If it is, the pins are the same and the perfboard harness can be used as-is in place of the breadboard mic wiring.

Verify in this order

Each step adds one thing, so a failure points at the thing just added.

  1. Board alone, on USB, nothing else connected. Flash ESPHome and watch the log. Expected: it boots, joins Wi-Fi and appears in HA's ESPHome integration. No serial device on xero means cable or port, not the board: xero has a dead USB port, and a charge-only cable lights the power LED but never enumerates (aarti runbook, "Phase 0").
  2. Rails before parts. Board on the breadboard with only its rail jumpers, USB in. With a meter against the GND rail: top + rail 3.2-3.35V; bottom + rail nothing; the VIN/5V pin 4.6-5.1V. Around 5V on the top rail means the rail jumper is on VIN; move it before the mic goes in. 3.3V on VIN means you have found the 3V3 pin under the wrong name.
  3. Mic in. Unplug USB, add the mic, plug back in. Say the wake word and a command, and open the run in HA's pipeline debug view (Settings, Voice assistants). Expected: a run from the satellite with your words as STT text. No runs at all, or runs with empty text, is the mic bus: WS and SCK swapped, L/R not on GND, or a YAML channel that does not match L/R. If the mic was on 5V at any point, suspect the mic itself.
  4. Stick in, set it red at low brightness from HA. Expected: all 8 red. Green instead of red = rgb_order in the YAML, not the wiring. Nothing lit = leads on the output end of the offcut, the 470R not in the DIN lead's row, or the stick's GND row not reaching the GND rail. First LED wrong or flickering, the rest right = logic level; fit the 74AHCT125.
  5. End to end. "Alejandro, turn on the hall light." Expected: the stick shows listening, the light switches, the stick shows done. Wake word never fires while a command typed in HA works = the openWakeWord side on xero, not this wiring.

Safe probingEvery check above is a voltage reading with the meter's probes on pins, or something read in HA. Nothing on this build is over 5V or over an amp. Do not bridge a supply pin to anything with a bare wire to "see if it lights": the one leg it can kill is the mic's VDD.
